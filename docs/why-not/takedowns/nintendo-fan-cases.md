# Nintendo Fan Cases (2015–2026)

Nintendo enforces harder than most, and often *after* release rather than before.
The pattern across its cases: asset reuse gets a clean claim, and anything an
official remake is about to cover becomes a target.

## Another Metroid 2 Remake (AM2R) — 2016

A free PC remake of *Metroid II* by **Milton Guasti (DoctorM64)**.

- Released **2016-08-06** after years of development.
- **2016-08-07**: Nintendo DMCA'd the download hosts.
- **2016-09-02**: a direct DMCA notice reached Guasti's email; development ended.
- Nintendo shipped its own **Metroid: Samus Returns** in 2017.
- In **September 2016** Nintendo DMCA'd **562 fan games** from Game Jolt.

## Pokémon Uranium — 2016

A nine-year fan game, released August 2016, with 1.5M+ downloads in days.

- Nintendo's lawyers sent takedown notices to the **hosting sites**, not directly
  to the developers.
- The creators **pre-emptively pulled their own download links** "out of respect"
  and received no C&D directly.

## Super Mario 64 HD — 2015

A Unity remake of the Bob-omb Battlefield by **Royston Ross**, playable in-browser.

- Taken down **at Nintendo's request** roughly a week after it drew attention.

## Contrast: Ocarina of Time UE remake (CryZENx) — self-cancelled, no letter

YouTuber **CryZENx** built an Unreal Engine remake of *Ocarina of Time* since 2016
(UE4, later UE5) and drew no enforcement for a decade. After **Nintendo announced
an official Ocarina of Time remake for Switch 2 (June 2026)**, CryZENx stopped the
project himself — "better to just stop the project for now or I would [have] been
disappeared… because of Nintendo ninjas." **No C&D was sent**; the old demos
remain downloadable.

This is the tell: enforcement often is not needed. When a publisher announces an
official version, the fan project either stops itself or gets stopped — and the
announcement alone is enough.
