# Takedowns — When Fan Projects Get Shut Down

A running collection of fan projects — mods, remakes, recompilations, private
servers — that rights holders shut down, and *why*. The pattern matters more than
any single case: the thing that draws fire is rarely the thing modders worry about.

> **Cross-reference**: rest of the miscellaneous section → [`../index.md`](../index.md).

---

## The pattern

The common belief is that you are safe if you:

- require a legitimate retail copy,
- ship no original assets or code,
- release for free, or
- avoid leaking anything.

Every one of these has failed in practice. The strongest predictor of a takedown
is not *how* the fan project is built but whether it **overlaps a live commercial
product** — a current release, a planned remaster, a competing online service, or
a publisher's own cancelled-then-revived ambitions.

Signals that reliably raise risk:

| Signal | Why it attracts attention |
|---|---|
| **Popularity spike** | A mod big enough to trend competes for the same attention as the official product. |
| **Monetization** | Donations, Patreon, shops, sponsor builds — even non-profit-adjacent money changes the legal picture. |
| **Online / private servers** | Emulated or private multiplayer directly substitutes for a paid service (Blizzard, MMO projects). |
| **A remaster or sequel in flight** | A fan remake of something the publisher is about to re-sell is competition, not homage. |
| **Asset reuse** | Ripped art, music, or code gives a clean claim, even when the project runs on a retail copy. |
| **Enforcement mood** | The same class of project is sometimes greenlit and sometimes axed; there is no consistent rule. |

Two things are worth separating:

- **A cease and desist letter** — a demand, usually complied with immediately.
- **A filed lawsuit / injunction** — a court outcome, with a permanent bar and
  sometimes money. Rare, but real (Turtle WoW).

## Takeaways

- **"We don't ship leaked content" is not protection.** H2M ran on a retail copy
  and shipped nothing ripped, and still took a pre-launch C&D.
- **Keep fan work in your own words and your own binaries.** Not because it is the
  only thing that draws fire, but because it removes the easy, uncontestable
  claim and narrows the dispute to competition and trademark.
- **Free is not a shield, and monetization is a beacon.** Removing money lowers
  risk; it does not remove it.
- **Announced official remakes are a countdown.** When a publisher revives
  something a fan project covers, expect the fan project to be the first casualty
  — either by letter or by self-cancellation.
- **Enforcement is arbitrary.** Identical projects are treated differently, so
  precedent is weak; a polite takedown is the cheapest exit.
